# Party House — Complete Rules and Character Reference

**Game:** *Party House*, game #25 in *UFO 50*  
**Document type:** Detailed gameplay rules, character reference, and interaction guide  
**Note:** This is an independently written explanation of the game mechanics, not an official rulebook. Character costs and interactions are based on community-documented gameplay; in-game behavior takes precedence.

## 1. The objective

You organize a series of house parties over **25 nights**. Your objective is to have **at least four Star guests attending the same party** before your final night is over. Buying four Star guests is not enough: they must be present together at one party. A party that is broken up by police or the fire marshal does not count as a successful party.

You build a **Rolodex**, a collection of guests who can be drawn to each party. You earn **Popularity (Pop)** to buy more guests and **Cash** to expand your house. You must manage the conflict between admitting more valuable guests and risking a failed party.

A standard game begins with:

- **25 nights** remaining.
- **House capacity of 5**.
- **4 Old Friends** (each generates 1 Pop).
- **2 Rich Pals** (each generates 1 Cash).
- **4 Wild Buddies** (each generates 2 Pop and 1 Trouble).

There are **five preset scenarios** and a **Random Scenario**, each offering a limited selection of guest types for purchase. Completing the five preset scenarios fulfills the standard completion goal. The Cherry goal requires five consecutive Random Scenario victories.

## 2. Structure of a night

Each night comprises a **Party Phase** followed by a **Planning Phase**.

### 2.1 Party Phase

1. The house begins empty, with its usual capacity.
2. Available Rolodex guests are eligible to enter, generally once each during the party unless returned to the pool by a special effect. A guest temporarily banned because of the previous night's police raid is unavailable.
3. Opening the front door admits a randomly selected available guest.
4. The new guest occupies a house space. Apply any automatic entrance or passive effect, including Trouble and effects that force more guests to arrive.
5. You may invite another guest, use an eligible guest's activated action, or voluntarily end the party.
6. When a party concludes successfully, score the resources of the guests still inside and any resources already awarded during the party.

You can end the party voluntarily even if there are more guests in the Rolodex and empty house spaces. When the house fills, you can still perform eligible actions before finishing, assuming the party has not already been forcibly terminated.

### 2.2 Planning Phase

After a successful party, you receive the earned Pop and Cash. You can spend:

- **Pop** to purchase guests, adding them permanently to the Rolodex.
- **Cash** to add house spaces, increasing the number of guests who can attend simultaneously.

The first expansion costs **2 Cash**, with each subsequent expansion costing **1 more**, capped at **12 Cash** per space. House capacity can reach **34**. Normal non-Star guests have a limit of four copies of a type, while Star guests are not subject to this four-copy limit. The starting Wild Buddies cannot be bought normally.

Guests cannot ordinarily be permanently removed from your Rolodex. Therefore, buying a guest improves one thing while also changing the probability of seeing every other guest on future nights.

## 3. Trouble, raids, and overcrowding

### 3.1 Trouble

Some guests have a **Trouble** symbol, represented by a red X. The party may contain up to **two net Trouble** safely. At **three net Trouble**, the police raid the house immediately.

On a police raid:

- The party immediately ends.
- It yields no Pop or Cash for that night.
- You choose one guest to take the blame; that guest is unavailable at the next party, then returns to the Rolodex afterward.

Guests such as Hippies, Cute Dogs, and Unicorns **cancel** Trouble while present. This reduces the net Trouble total but usually does not erase the underlying Trouble properties. By contrast, a Counselor can **clear** Trouble properties for the rest of the party. This matters for Writers and Bartenders, which benefit from the number of guests that still possess Trouble.

**Example:** Four Wild Buddies (+4 Trouble) and two Cute Dogs (−2 Trouble) yield **2 net Trouble**, a safe party. A Writer still sees four troublemakers and gains its corresponding bonus.

### 3.2 Overcrowding

Your house cannot accommodate more people than its capacity. Some guests force other guests to arrive upon entrance. If these chained or forced arrivals push attendance **above capacity**, the **fire marshal** ends the party without a payout. Occupying exactly the available capacity is fine.

**Example:** If four people are already in a five-person house, admitting Mr. Popular fills the fifth space and triggers another forced guest, causing overcrowding.

### 3.3 Cash shortfalls

Some characters have negative Cash contributions. When a party's Cash scoring cannot cover such costs, the unpaid amount incurs a **7 Pop penalty per Cash** shortfall. Thus, the ability to pay guests is important even in a strategy focused primarily on Popularity.

## 4. Ability terminology and timing

- **Score:** Receive a guest's Pop/Cash contribution, including any conditional bonuses in effect at that moment.
- **Immediate score:** Some activated actions score guests during the party. The same guest can score again at a successful party's conclusion if still present.
- **Fetch / summon:** Bring a specific available guest from the Rolodex rather than accepting an ordinary random door draw.
- **Peek:** Inspect the next guest, then admit or reject them. A rejected guest is excluded for that party.
- **Boot / evict:** Remove a guest from the house for the rest of the current party. This frees capacity and removes their ongoing effects.
- **Send everyone outside / shuffle:** Return the current guests to the available invitation pool, allowing them to be drawn again. Excluded/booted guests do not return, and used activated actions ordinarily remain spent.
- **Bring:** An automatic arrival effect triggered when a guest enters. Brought guests can themselves trigger further brings.
- **Refresh:** Restore other eligible guests' used activated actions; Cheerleaders cannot refresh Cheerleaders.
- **Passive:** Applies while a guest is in the party, or at a specified scoring event.
- **Permanent bonus:** Persists across subsequent nights for that individual guest, including upgrades from a Stylist or repeated arrivals of a Climber.

Activated actions generally may be used **once per individual guest per night**. A guest's action is not refreshed merely by sending them outside and readmitting them. A Cheerleader's refresh is a notable exception. Actions cannot save you once a forced party-ending raid has already occurred.

## 5. Complete roster: 46 guests

The costs below are in **Popularity**. A character's scoring contributions are different from its purchase cost. “−1 Cash” is a fee at scoring time, not a price in the shop.

### 5.1 Starting and inexpensive guests

#### 1. Old Friend — Cost: 2 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop.
- **Ability:** None.
- **Details:** A reliable but low-output starting guest. The Mascot receives an extra Pop for each Old Friend attending.

#### 2. Rich Pal — Cost: 3 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Cash.
- **Ability:** None.
- **Details:** A basic early source of the Cash needed to expand the house. Produces no base Pop.

#### 3. Wild Buddy — Starting guest; not normally purchasable
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop.
- **Trouble:** +1.
- **Details:** Four begin in the Rolodex. They are useful for Pop and for Writer/Bartender bonuses but create much of the starting raid risk.

#### 4. Driver — Cost: 3 Pop
- **Scoring:** None.
- **Action — Fetch:** Choose an available Rolodex guest to enter directly.
- **Details:** Selectively acquires Stars or crucial support guests and reduces dependence on random draws. The fetched guest still occupies space and can trigger entrance effects.

#### 5. Monkey — Cost: 3 Pop
- **Scoring:** +4 Pop.
- **Trouble:** +1.
- **Details:** Exceptional early Pop efficiency, balanced by increased police risk. Complements Trouble cancellation and Writer.

#### 6. Security — Cost: 4 Pop
- **Scoring:** None.
- **Action — Boot:** Remove one attending guest for the remainder of this party, including Security itself if desired.
- **Details:** Useful for clearing a troublemaker or replacing a weak guest with another draw.

#### 7. Private Eye — Cost: 4 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop, −1 Cash.
- **Action — Fetch:** Choose one available guest to enter.
- **Details:** A Driver that generates some Pop but has an ongoing Cash cost. Excellent for consistently collecting specific Stars.

#### 8. Ticket Taker — Cost: 4 Pop
- **Scoring:** −1 Pop, +2 Cash.
- **Ability:** None.
- **Details:** Converts a little Pop production into valuable early Cash for house upgrades.

#### 9. Introvert — Cost: 4 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop plus +1 Pop per empty house space when scored.
- **Ability:** Conditional scoring.
- **Details:** Rewards voluntarily finishing a party with unused space. For instance, with capacity 10 and six occupants, one Introvert produces 5 Pop.

#### 10. Watch Dog — Cost: 4 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop.
- **Action — Peek:** Inspect the next guest and admit or reject them.
- **Details:** Can prevent a third Trouble from entering or reject a guest who would overcrowd the house.

#### 11. Hippy — Cost: 4 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop.
- **Passive:** Cancels 1 Trouble while present.
- **Details:** Makes troublesome guests safer without stripping their Trouble properties, preserving Writer/Bartender synergy.

### 5.2 Five-Pop guests

#### 12. Photographer — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop, −1 Cash.
- **Action — Photograph:** Choose another attending guest and score that guest immediately.
- **Details:** The subject can still score at the end of the party. Particularly strong when the target has high conditional or permanent Pop, such as Dancer, Writer, or a fully improved Climber.

#### 13. Cheerleader — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop.
- **Action — Refresh:** Restore spent abilities of other eligible non-Cheerleader guests currently present.
- **Details:** Enables second Fetches, Photographs, Boots, Stylist improvements, etc. Cheerleaders cannot refresh one another.

#### 14. Rock Star — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +3 Pop, +2 Cash.
- **Trouble:** +1.
- **Details:** High income in both resources at the cost of risk. Best when supported by Trouble mitigation.

#### 15. Comedian — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** −1 Cash; +5 Pop **only when the house is full** at scoring time.
- **Details:** A strong reward for full occupancy and a poor reward when ending early. Photographing a Comedian also depends on fullness at the moment of scoring.

#### 16. Caterer — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +4 Pop, −1 Cash.
- **Details:** Provides a consistent large Pop contribution without needing a full house, but requires sufficient Cash income.

#### 17. Mr. Popular — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +3 Pop.
- **Entrance:** Automatically brings one additional available random guest.
- **Details:** Produces free extra attendance but can cause overcrowding or a chain of bring effects. Allow spare room before admitting.

#### 18. Grillmaster — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop.
- **Action — Send everyone outside:** Return the current house guests to the available Rolodex pool.
- **Details:** Rebuilds an unsatisfactory party or retriggers entrance abilities; used actions do not reset automatically. Previously booted/rejected guests stay unavailable.

#### 19. Mascot — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop plus +1 additional Pop per Old Friend present.
- **Details:** Multiple Mascots each count the same Old Friends. Makes the basic Old Friend cards useful in a specialized composition.

#### 20. Werewolf — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +4 Pop.
- **Entrance:** Alternates between a harmless human form and a wolf form contributing +1 Trouble.
- **Details:** First arrival changes it from its starting human state into wolf form; subsequent entrances toggle again. Sending it outside and readmitting it can change its Trouble status.

#### 21. Greeter — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop.
- **Action — Greet:** Bring in the next guest and immediately score the newly arrived guest(s).
- **Details:** Especially rewarding with strong Pop/Cash guests and bring chains. The arrivals still occupy real capacity and create Trouble normally.

#### 22. Magician — Cost: 5 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop.
- **Action — Swap:** Select an attendee and replace them with an available random guest of the opposite Star status: non-Star becomes Star, or Star becomes non-Star.
- **Details:** The selected attendee leaves for the rest of this party. If no eligible replacement exists, the exchange cannot occur. This is highly valuable for completing the four-Star objective.

### 5.3 Midrange guests

#### 23. Gangster — Cost: 6 Pop
- **Scoring:** +4 Cash.
- **Trouble:** +1.
- **Details:** Very high flat Cash production; dangerous without Trouble mitigation.

#### 24. Athlete — Cost: 6 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop, +1 Cash.
- **Action:** Send everyone outside, as with Grillmaster.
- **Details:** Trades one point of Grillmaster Pop for one point of Cash and costs more to acquire.

#### 25. Cute Dog — Cost: 7 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop.
- **Passive:** Cancels 1 Trouble.
- **Details:** A stronger but more expensive version of Hippy. Multiple copies can cancel several Trouble points.

#### 26. Gambler — Cost: 7 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop, +3 Cash.
- **Trouble:** +1.
- **Details:** Balanced resource production with the usual risk of a red-X guest.

#### 27. Dancer — Cost: 7 Pop
- **Scoring:** With **D** Dancers in the party, each Dancer scores **D² Pop**.
- **Details:** One Dancer scores 1; two score 4 each (8 total); three score 9 each (27 total); four score 16 each (64 total). Photographers can take advantage of the high per-Dancer payout.

#### 28. Stylist — Cost: 7 Pop
- **Scoring:** −1 Cash.
- **Action — Style:** Permanently add +1 Pop to one attending guest.
- **Details:** The improvement is attached to that specific guest across nights, and bonuses can stack. Refreshing the Stylist with a Cheerleader allows additional upgrades.

#### 29. Counselor — Cost: 7 Pop
- **Scoring:** None.
- **Action — Calm:** Clear the Trouble property from currently attending troublemakers for the rest of that party.
- **Details:** Offers strong Trouble protection, but guests whose Trouble is cleared no longer contribute to Writer or Bartender's count.

#### 30. Cupid — Cost: 8 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop.
- **Action — Boot pair:** Remove two horizontally adjacent guests for the rest of the party.
- **Details:** They must be adjacent to each other, not necessarily to Cupid. Cupid can be one of the two guests. Cannot remove only one.

#### 31. Spy — Cost: 8 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Cash.
- **Action — Peek:** Inspect and accept/reject the next guest.
- **Details:** Combines dependable Cash with risk and lineup control.

#### 32. Writer — Cost: 8 Pop
- **Scoring:** +1 Pop plus +2 Pop per attending guest retaining the Trouble property.
- **Details:** Counts troublemakers even when their Trouble is merely cancelled by Hippy, Cute Dog or Unicorn. Does not count guests booted or whose Trouble is cleared by Counselor.

### 5.4 Premium non-Star guests

#### 33. Auctioneer — Cost: 9 Pop
- **Scoring:** +3 Cash.
- **Details:** A high-value, predictable Cash guest without Trouble or action requirements.

#### 34. Wrestler — Cost: 9 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop.
- **Action — Boot:** Remove one attendee for the remainder of the party.
- **Details:** Security's action combined with positive Pop income. May boot itself.

#### 35. Celebrity — Cost: 11 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Pop, +3 Cash.
- **Entrance:** Brings **two** additional guests.
- **Details:** Strong resources but a substantial overcrowding risk. If brought guests themselves bring others, the forced arrivals chain; always leave spare room.

#### 36. Bartender — Cost: 11 Pop
- **Scoring:** +2 Cash per attending guest retaining the Trouble property.
- **Details:** The Cash counterpart to Writer. Ordinary Trouble cancellation preserves the count; Counselor's Trouble removal reduces it. There is no guaranteed extra base Pop in the documented character statistics.

#### 37. Climber — Cost: 12 Pop
- **Scoring:** Permanently gains +1 Pop each time it **enters** the party, up to a maximum of **9 Pop**.
- **Details:** Progress carries between nights. Each new entrance after a Grillmaster/Athlete shuffle can add another point. Especially good when purchased early.

### 5.5 All nine Star guests

Every Star guest below counts as **one Star** while attending, regardless of its other properties. Four Stars present together in a successfully completed party are the win condition.

#### 38. Dinosaur — Cost: 25 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Trouble:** +1.
- **Other scoring:** None.
- **Details:** Cheapest Star, but four Dinosaurs are unsafe without substantial mitigation. Can be a bargain alongside Unicorn or Counselor.

#### 39. Dragon — Cost: 30 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Scoring:** −3 Cash.
- **Details:** A comparatively cheap Star with a substantial Cash burden on ordinary scored parties. The Cash shortfall rule can make repeated early Dragon attendance expensive.

#### 40. Mermaid — Cost: 35 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Entrance:** Brings one additional random guest.
- **Details:** Offers a mid-price Star but risks forced overcrowding. A brought guest can bring more guests, so allow capacity for chains.

#### 41. Alien — Cost: 40 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Ability/other scoring:** None.
- **Details:** A neutral Star: no Trouble, no fees and no supporting abilities. Straightforward, but offers no economic value before winning.

#### 42. Unicorn — Cost: 45 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Passive:** Cancels 1 Trouble.
- **Details:** A Star that actively helps accommodate troublemakers and Dinosaurs. Cancellation preserves Writer/Bartender synergies.

#### 43. Ghost — Cost: 45 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Action — Boot:** Remove one attendee for the rest of the party.
- **Details:** Can make room for another Star, evict Trouble, or evict itself when you are still developing your economy.

#### 44. Superhero — Cost: 50 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Scoring:** +3 Pop.
- **Details:** A safe Star that continues to generate some Popularity on parties before victory.

#### 45. Leprechaun — Cost: 50 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Scoring:** +3 Cash.
- **Details:** Generates resources for late house expansions while contributing toward your Star count.

#### 46. Genie — Cost: 55 Pop
- **Star:** 1.
- **Action — Summon:** Choose one available guest to enter directly.
- **Details:** The most expensive Star, but can fetch another Star. Several Genies can summon one another to assemble four Stars without relying on all four entering randomly.

## 6. Important interactions and worked examples

### 6.1 Four Dancers

With four Dancers present, each earns **4² = 16 Pop**, totaling **64 Pop**. If a Photographer scores a Dancer while all four are present, that yields another **16 Pop immediately**, subject to the Photographer's own Cash fee when scored.

### 6.2 Trouble cancellation versus Trouble removal

Suppose the house contains four Wild Buddies, two Cute Dogs and one Writer.

- The Wild Buddies have **4 Trouble** combined.
- The Cute Dogs cancel **2**, giving **2 net Trouble**: safe.
- The Writer still counts all four Wild Buddies and scores **1 + 2×4 = 9 Pop**.

If a Counselor instead clears the Wild Buddies' Trouble properties, the Writer loses those four qualifying troublemakers from its calculation.

### 6.3 The Climber loop

You admit Climber (+1 permanent Pop), then use a Grillmaster or Athlete to send the house outside. If the Climber returns later in the same party, its value becomes +2; another arrival raises it to +3, and so on, up to +9. The growth remains even if you finish the party later or suffer a raid.

### 6.4 Action refresh loop

A Driver fetches a valuable guest and uses its action. A Cheerleader then refreshes the Driver; the Driver can fetch another available guest. A Photographer can similarly be refreshed to score an additional guest. The Cheerleader cannot refresh another Cheerleader.

### 6.5 Star assembly

Once your Rolodex contains at least four Stars, your central problem is finding all four in one party. You can improve your chances by:

1. Expanding the house, giving you more draw opportunities and room for summoned guests.
2. Using Drivers, Private Eyes and Genies to choose missing Stars directly.
3. Using Magicians to swap ordinary attendees for available Stars.
4. Using Ghosts, Security, Wrestlers or Cupid to remove non-Stars and free space.
5. Using Watch Dogs and Spies to reject unwanted next arrivals.

Do not keep drawing after a safe four-Star win is available: end the party rather than risk a raid or overcrowding.

## 7. Progression modes and special features

- **Preset scenarios:** Five curated challenges with restricted guest selections.
- **Random Scenario:** Randomized selection of available guests; successive victories matter for the Cherry requirement.
- **Cherry goal:** Win five consecutive Random Scenarios.
- **Terminal codes:** `VIPS-ONLY` unlocks custom scenario options; `MAKE-GLEE` permits entry of scenario codes. Using terminal codes invalidates the current session for records, achievements and unlockables.

## 8. Quick rules checklist

1. You have 25 nights to host one party containing four or more Stars.
2. Your Rolodex is the collection from which guests enter randomly.
3. Every attending guest normally occupies one house space.
4. Ordinary Trouble reaches a raid at three net Trouble.
5. Raids and overcrowding cancel that party's resources.
6. Pop purchases guests; Cash expands the house.
7. Most actions are once per guest per party, unless refreshed.
8. Booted and rejected guests are excluded from the remainder of that party.
9. Automatic bring effects can chain and cause overcrowding.
10. Permanent guest improvements survive between parties.
11. Four purchased Stars are insufficient; four must attend together.
12. The final party must succeed before the 25-night limit expires.
